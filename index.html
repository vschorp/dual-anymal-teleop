Abstract

Robotic mobile manipulators hold significant potential to enhance safety and efficiency across a wide range of tasks in difficult or remote environments. While full autonomy remains a challenge due to the complexity of real-world scenarios, teleoperation offers a practical alternative by incorporating human intelligence into remote robotic systems. This paper introduces a teleoperated bimanual system that coordinates two independent, mobile, single-arm robots. A movable arm-mounted stereo camera provides dynamic and adjustable viewpoints, enhancing the operator's depth perception and enabling precise manipulation even in challenging scenarios, such as inserting a plug, pouring liquid from one container to another, or opening a backpack. Using a surgical teleoperation platform allows force feedback integration, further enhancing operator control, offering tangible haptic awareness, and enabling fine-grained interaction with the environment.

In Action

Collect

To eliminate operational blind spots, the robots utilize a specialized sensor suite to capture a complete digital snapshot of critical data center infrastructure. Moving beyond simple visual checks, the system collects:

  • Thermal Signatures: High-precision FLIR sensors map heat distribution across server racks and electrical switchgear to identify early-stage thermal bridging or overheating.
  • Visual Telemetry: High-resolution RGB cameras capture hardware status indicators, structural anomalies, and physical wear.
  • Acoustic Profiling: High-fidelity audio sensors listen to the mechanical rhythms of cooling units and power systems, picking up on the subtle frequency changes that precede mechanical failure.
  • Environmental Metrics: Continuous air temperature probing ensures ambient conditions remain optimal at every level of the infrastructure.

Analyse

The collected data is analyzed using a time series aggregation framework and anomaly detection models.

Sound

The sound samples are analyzed in the frequency domain. When the fans are turned on the peak around 9 kHz is clearly visibile which correspond to the fan blade frequency (6800 rpm = 113 Hz, 80 blades => 9 kHz). When the fan is turned off, the peak disappears and the spectrum is dominated by the noise floor.

Predict

We believe that preventing data center outages is fundamentally a data challenge, not a hardware one. That is why our core focus is our on-premise reasoning AI, which translates subtle field anomalies into actionable maintenance. We feed technical manuals into a RAG pipeline powered by reasoning models, allowing the system to cross-reference data outliers against the machine's own documentation to map exact causality chains and pinpoint the technical root cause.
